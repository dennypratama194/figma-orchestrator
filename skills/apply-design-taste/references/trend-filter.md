# Trend filter

Trends change. Research them near the time of the project instead of encoding a permanent style list.

## Research method

1. Inspect recent, real, shipped work relevant to the industry, audience, and medium. Include live products and respected curated sources; do not rely only on concept galleries.
2. Record the observation date and source links.
3. Extract principles such as hierarchy, navigation behavior, type scale, motion, image treatment, density, or composition. Do not copy a page layout.
4. Compare each principle against Brand DNA, user expectations, content, accessibility, performance, and implementation constraints.
5. Classify it as `adopt`, `adapt`, `avoid`, or `irrelevant`, with one sentence of evidence.

## Adoption test

A trend earns `adopt` or `adapt` only when it does at least one of the following without harming another:

- improves comprehension or interaction;
- strengthens credible brand distinction;
- gives important content a more fitting expression;
- matches an emerging audience convention worth recognizing;
- creates memorable narrative or product demonstration.

“It looks modern” is insufficient evidence.

## Freshness without trend chasing

Prefer durable fundamentals plus one or two current signals. Avoid stacking several fashionable treatments. If removing the trend leaves no meaningful concept, the direction is underdeveloped.

Do not call a style current based solely on model memory. When currentness matters, gather dated evidence and state uncertainty.
